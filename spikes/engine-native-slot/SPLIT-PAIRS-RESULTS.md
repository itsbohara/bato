# Bato split pairs

Date: 2026-11-01. Status: bounded application/runtime acceptance passed; pointer divider dragging and broader focus acceptance remain open. This report does not treat earlier Chromium split experiments as acceptance of the Bato ownership bridge.

## Implemented behavior

A workspace stores ordered `PageSplit` relationships with stable IDs, left/right page IDs, divider ratio and pinned status. Complete pages remain canonical in `Workspace.pins` and `Workspace.tabs`; Favorites are excluded. Members must be distinct, local to the workspace, and belong to at most one pair. Ratios must be finite and strictly between zero and one. Missing split fields decode to an empty list and no active pair; encoding omits empty/default split fields. The organization document remains version 1.

`activeSplitID` selects the pair and the existing `activeEntryID` records its focused side. Selecting a member retains that pair; selecting an unrelated page exits it. Unpinned pairs are discarded when separated or replaced by another selection/pair. Pinned relationships remain available after separation. Pin Pair converts temporary members to canonical pins using their latest committed destinations while preserving existing saved destinations. A saved pair reopens each member's saved URL with fresh process-local generations as needed. No Chromium tab or split identifier is serialized.

Closing a loaded member still waits for confirmed closure and respects beforeunload cancellation. Closing a saved member unloads/resets it, exits the displayed pair and leaves the survivor usable; the pinned relationship remains available for reopening. Removing or transferring a member removes its relationship. Unsolicited teardown unload preserves committed pair/selection metadata for restart rather than recreating pages during shutdown. Generation checks reject stale observations and terminal events. Split creation commits organization before engine effects; rejected engine work leaves committed entries available for retry. Failed saves do not publish the candidate or initiate requested page work.

The native sidebar keeps page rows and folder membership intact. A page context action, **Split with Current Page**, captures the active source/workspace and revalidates them when invoked. An active split strip offers left/right focus, **Pin pair** and **Separate**. Saved pair rows reopen a pair; **Remove Split Pair** removes only its relationship and keeps both pages. Controls have stable accessibility IDs and restore native focus across rerenders. Modal/pending-command guards apply; pair controls disable while either member closes.

## Native Chromium bridge

The pinned engine remains CEF `156.0.14+g4fa4ab5`, revision `4fa4ab516dacf6c49fc7e6e70d2b7ebce29fa35f`, with Chromium `156.0.8078.48`, commit `d0c02bfad08ec92a6ffcf989e53cd995e6381a4c`. Three authored exports register split observations, present a pair, and separate the managed pair. They supplement the existing native-slot interface; the original WorkspaceConfigure signature remains unchanged. This is direct native `TabStripModel` integration, without a controller extension or a pair of independent CEF windows.

Swift resolves exact `LivePage` generations to bound CEF browsers. The client checks the owned slot, pending-close state, browser validity, same window and explicitly bound request context. Engine mutation uses the existing guarded slot lookup and checks editable tab-strip state, matching native pin/group state and pair membership. It creates/reuses a native side-by-side split, preserves requested left/right order, applies the ratio and activates the requested side. It refuses to adopt unrelated native splits. Separation removes only the exact managed pair. Reentrant model operations recheck slot ownership and membership; mutation suppression prevents command-driven observations from recursively changing organization.

Native ratio observations are copied out of tab-model callbacks and delivered on the CEF UI task runner after a 200 ms debounce. Slot revision, observation sequence, browser identity and exact Swift live generations reject stale deliveries. The runtime saves the observed ratio without re-presenting the split. Native pair removal is observed separately; member-close handling remains with the existing confirmed page lifecycle. Chromium owns divider input, page views, focused-side routing, toolbar/origin/security UI and extension anchors.

## Limits

This milestone supports two-page side-by-side pairs only. Favorites, nested layouts and Peek are outside its scope. Organization Profile IDs do not establish separate engine storage; the current adapter still binds one explicitly supported engine context. Menu-driven divider ratio persistence and restoration passed. Native observed-divider callbacks have source/test coverage only. Earlier keyboard divider resizing passed in the separate native-slot experiment; neither pointer dragging nor full keyboard traversal passed in this Bato run. The Tab attempt stayed at the address field without establishing a cause. No general focus-trap cause/fix is claimed. Equal Width/Widen Right menu commands, external native split removal/swap, disjoint saved-pair replacement and all control focus roles are source/test-reviewed rather than separately runtime-accepted here. Fullscreen, rapid resize, multiwindow/modal combinations, real-extension focused-pane behavior and broader accessibility acceptance remain separate gates. No extension matrix was requested or rerun for this work. Existing rendering/lifecycle observations in earlier reports remain open unless separately resolved; no daily-use or production-readiness claim follows.
